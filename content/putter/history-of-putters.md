# Putter History and the Anser Design

[[index|Home]] · [[Putter/index|Putter]] · [[references|References]]

## A Small Stroke with a Large Design Story

The putter has a different job from clubs built mainly to send the ball through the air. Putting emphasizes starting direction and distance control, so even a relatively compact head offers several design questions. Where should the weight sit? What should the golfer see behind the ball? How should the shaft connect to the head?

## An Influential Milestone

PING dates the Anser design to January 1966. Its heel-and-toe weighting and recognizable layout became an influential part of modern putter history. George Archer used an Anser when he won the 1969 Masters, connecting the design to a major championship success. PING's heritage account documents these milestones. [1]

### Why the Story Matters

The Anser provides a clear example of solving a practical problem through head design. Rather than focusing only on appearance, clubmakers considered how mass placement could affect performance. That same broad idea also appears in the development of perimeter-weighted irons.

Later putters expanded the range of shapes available to golfers. Compact blades and larger mallets offer different visual and physical layouts, but both still serve the same basic task on the green.

> Historical takeaway: a familiar shape can survive while its materials and details continue to change.

This original observation helps explain why an older putter outline may resemble a modern model without the two clubs being identical in construction or feel.
