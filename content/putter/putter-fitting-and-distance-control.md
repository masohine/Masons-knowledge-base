# Putter Fitting and Distance Control

[[index|Home]] · [[Putter/index|Putter]] · [[references|References]]

## Match the Setup to the Player

Putter fitting considers how the club works with the golfer's posture and stroke. Length, lie, loft, head weight, and shaft connection can all enter the discussion. Scotty Cameron's putting guide explains these as related selection factors rather than treating head shape as the only decision. [1]

A putter's job includes more than pointing at the hole. The golfer must also send the ball an appropriate distance. A comfortable setup can help make practice repeatable, but equipment cannot read the green or choose the pace on its own.

## A Practical Way to Compare

Imagine evaluating two putters on the same practice green. Instead of counting only the putts that drop, compare the quality of the misses as well. A putter that leaves a smaller spread around the target may be worth investigating even if a short trial produces the same number of holed putts.

### A Simple Practice Record

1. Use the same ball type during the comparison.
2. Try several short and longer putts.
3. Note starting direction and finishing distance separately.
4. Repeat the comparison rather than relying on one excellent stroke.

This is an illustrative evaluation routine, not a formal fitting protocol. Its purpose is to separate aiming from pace and notice repeatable patterns. The development of fitting adds another chapter to putter history: increasingly detailed choices are useful only when they connect to what the golfer actually does.
