## Clubs Do Not Fit Everyone the Same Way

Iron fitting reflects a shift from treating every golfer as the same size and shape to considering individual differences. PING describes using measurements such as height, wrist-to-floor distance, and hand size when establishing club length, lie angle, and grip size. These measurements provide useful information, but fitting also needs to consider how the golfer actually delivers the club. [1]

## Understanding the Main Terms

**Length** affects the dimensions of the club the golfer must control. **Lie angle** describes the relationship between the shaft and the sole when the club is positioned for measurement. **Grip size** changes the handle the player holds. None of these terms should be confused with loft, which relates to the face angle in the vertical plane.

### Evaluate the Whole Pattern

Imagine two golfers of similar height who stand differently and deliver the club differently. It would be reasonable to investigate whether identical clubs suit both players rather than assume height settles the question.

A useful comparison looks at several outcomes together:

1. Where contact occurs on the face.
2. Whether shots start consistently.
3. Whether carry distances form useful gaps.
4. Whether the golfer can use the setup comfortably.

The historical importance of fitting is that progress is not limited to a new material or head shape. Better matching between equipment and player can also change how a familiar type of club is used.
