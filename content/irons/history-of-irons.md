# The Development of Golf Irons

[[index|Home]] · [[Irons/index|Irons]] · [[references|References]]

## A Club Family Built Around Control

Irons occupy much of the space between long-distance clubs and specialized short-game equipment. Their development shows a continuing effort to balance shot control with help on imperfect contact. Looking at the back of an iron can reveal part of this story: some heads have a compact, solid appearance, while others have an obvious cavity or a more complex construction.

## The Influence of Perimeter Weighting

PING introduced irons using perimeter weighting in 1969. Moving more mass toward the outside of the head helped establish an approach associated with cavity-back irons. This was an important development because it treated mishits as a design problem rather than assuming every player would strike the exact center. [1]

### More Than One Direction of Progress

Later iron development did not erase all earlier shapes. Compact irons, cavity backs, and other constructions can exist alongside one another because golfers value different combinations of appearance, feedback, and forgiveness. PING's G730 description, for example, emphasizes a larger, forgiving design and the role of fitting. [2]

A useful historical comparison asks what problem a clubmaker was trying to solve. Was the goal to change how the head twists, help the ball launch, or create a preferred shape at address? Answering those questions makes the equipment easier to understand than ranking clubs only by age.

This history also connects with putters, where weight moved toward the heel and toe became an influential design idea.
