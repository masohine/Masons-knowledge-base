## How the Head Is Made

Forging and casting describe manufacturing processes. In forging, metal is shaped under pressure, often through multiple stages. In casting, molten metal is poured into a mold and allowed to solidify. Mizuno's explanation of its forging process provides an example of how a manufacturer controls the material and shaping of an iron head. [1]

These terms are different from blade and cavity back, which describe a club's design. Mixing the two sets of labels can make golf equipment seem more confusing than it needs to be.

## Process Is Only Part of the Story

A golfer may associate forged clubs with a certain feel, but a manufacturing label is not a complete performance report. Head shape, material, face construction, shaft, ball, and contact all contribute to what a player experiences. A useful comparison should therefore include actual shots rather than relying only on the word stamped in a catalog.

### Questions for Comparing Irons

- What is the shape and construction of the head?
- How does the club respond to the golfer's normal contact?
- Are the distances consistent?
- Does the club suit the player's preferences at address?

From a historical perspective, manufacturing methods matter because they help explain how clubmakers turn a design into a repeatable product. For the player, however, the practical question remains whether that finished club performs its intended job. Understanding the process adds context without requiring the golfer to treat one method as automatically superior.
