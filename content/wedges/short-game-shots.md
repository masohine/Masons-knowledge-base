# Wedges and Short-Game Decisions

[[index|Home]] · [[Wedges/index|Wedges]] · [[references|References]]

## One Area, Several Different Shots

The short game includes more than one kind of movement toward the hole. A chip generally uses a lower flight with more of the journey on the ground. A pitch usually carries farther through the air. A greenside bunker shot introduces sand between the club and ball. These broad descriptions help explain why wedge design includes different lofts and sole shapes.

## Choose a Landing Plan

Consider a ball just off the green with open ground between it and the hole. A low shot might be one option. If a bunker blocks the route, more carry may be needed. These examples show why selecting a club starts with the situation rather than automatically reaching for the highest-lofted wedge.

### Questions Before the Shot

- What must the ball carry over?
- Where could it land?
- How much room is available for it to roll?
- What does the lie allow the golfer to do reliably?

Vokey's grind guide describes sole designs intended for different shot preferences and conditions. That supports the broader idea that versatility depends on matching the tool to the task. [1]

A wedge collection tells the history of those tasks becoming more specialized. Yet having more options also makes decision-making important. Near the green, the goal is a manageable next shot, and sometimes that means considering a simpler option instead of the most dramatic flight.
