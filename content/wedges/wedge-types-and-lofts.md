## Four Names, Different Roles

Wedges are commonly grouped as pitching, gap, sand, and lob wedges. The names describe familiar roles, but actual lofts vary across models. A pitching wedge usually connects to the iron set. A gap wedge helps cover the space before the higher-lofted wedges. Sand and lob wedges give players additional options near the green.

## Build a Useful Progression

Vokey recommends approximately four to six degrees of loft separation as a starting point for wedge gapping. This is guidance rather than a guarantee of identical distance gaps. The golfer's swing and the particular club still influence the result. [1]

### An Illustrative Setup



A set with those lofts would look evenly spaced on paper. However, the golfer would still need to compare typical carry distances to see whether the arrangement works. Two clubs can have a neat numerical gap without producing the desired shot separation.

The most useful starting question is what loft the existing pitching wedge has. From there, the golfer can connect the wedges to the rest of the bag. This avoids treating each wedge as an isolated purchase and shows how specialized equipment becomes part of an organized set.
