## Bounce: How the Sole Meets the Ground

Bounce describes the angle associated with the relationship between the leading edge and the lower part of the sole. It helps explain why a wedge can resist digging. Vokey's guide connects bounce selection with both the golfer's delivery and playing conditions. More bounce can be useful in softer conditions or with a steeper delivery, while less may suit other situations. These are general tendencies, not universal rules. [1]

## Grind: Shaping the Sole

Grind refers to the shape of the sole, including areas where material has been removed or reshaped. Vokey offers different grinds to change turf interaction and the options available when the face is opened or closed. A grind therefore adds information that a loft number cannot provide. [2]

### Compare the Whole Design

Two wedges can share a loft but respond differently because their soles differ. Think of a golfer comparing a wide-soled wedge with one that has more material removed from the heel and trailing edge. The useful question is how each behaves from the lies that golfer commonly faces.

This is why choosing a wedge only by its largest stamped number leaves out part of the story. Loft helps organize the set, while bounce and grind describe other aspects of the club's work.

The sand wedge's history introduced the importance of surface interaction. Modern grind choices continue that idea by offering more ways to adapt a familiar club category to different situations.
