# How Wikipedia Organizes Information

For this analysis, I chose **Wikipedia**, a public knowledge base that helps people explore many different subjects. I focused on its golf content because it shows how one broad topic connects to more specific information about equipment, rules, and players.

## 1. What Resources Are Being Organized?

Wikipedia mainly organizes encyclopedia articles. Each article focuses on a subject, while images, tables, and references help explain or support the information. For example, the [Golf article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf) includes sections about the sport's history, equipment, and scoring. The table of contents menu lets readers jump directly to these sections.

The resources being organized are descriptions of golf and its related topics, rather than the physical clubs or balls themselves. Separating subjects into individual articles makes it possible to explain details without putting everything on one huge page.

## 2. What Interactions Does the System Support?

Wikipedia supports several useful activities:

- **Research:** Readers can begin with an overview before exploring a narrower topic.
- **Browsing:** Links allow readers to discover related subjects without starting another search.
- **Fact checking:** References provide a way to look for the sources behind statements.

For example, someone researching golf equipment could start with the Golf article and follow its link to golf clubs. The article also has references and a page history, which help readers investigate its sources and changes. However, a citation is a starting point for checking a claim, not automatic proof that the claim is correct. These features are visible on the [Golf page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf).

## 3. What Classification System Does It Use?

Wikipedia uses **hierarchical categories with overlapping membership**. Broad categories contain narrower subcategories, but a resource can connect to more than one broader group. Wikipedia's [category guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Category) explains this structure.

The [Golf equipment category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Golf_equipment), for example, includes subcategories for golf clubs, equipment manufacturers, and retailers. It also belongs to both Golf and Sports equipment by sport. This gives readers more than one route to the same subject.

### Is It Effective?

I think this works well for students and casual readers because they can search directly or explore related groups. However, the equipment category mixes objects, businesses, and facilities, such as golf balls and driving ranges. That variety can make the boundaries feel unclear. It works, but less predictable than a system with strict, separate categories.

---

## 4. How Are Relationships Represented?

Wikipedia represents relationships through hyperlinks, category membership, and sections such as **See also**. In the Golf article, links to golf balls and clubs connect the sport to its equipment. Category pages group related resources, while references connect statements to outside sources.

These connections serve different purposes. A category shows shared membership, while an inline link provides context. For my own knowledge base, this is a useful example of how navigation can help readers move from a general interest to a specific question.
