# From Metal Spikes to Spikeless Designs

## Footwear Becomes Part of Golf Equipment

Golf shoes must support both walking and swinging. Players need comfort over a long round, but they also need traction while shifting their weight and rotating. Traditional golf footwear often combined leather uppers with metal spikes. The spikes helped anchor the golfer to the ground, although they could also leave marks on putting surfaces.

FootJoy's company history show that its shoes were selected for the American Ryder Cup team in 1927. This milestone illustrates how specialized footwear had become part of competitive golf equipment by the early twentieth century.

## The Move Toward Softer Spikes

Softspikes traces its replaceable spike technology to 1993. Nonmetal cleats became an important alternative to traditional metal spikes, combining traction with a design intended to be gentler on course surfaces. This change showed a broader design challenge, equipment needed to serve the golfer while also considering the playing environment.

### Modern Spikeless Shoes

Spikeless shoes use molded traction patterns instead of separate replaceable spikes. Golfers can choose between these soles and cleated designs according to comfort, fit, and playing conditions. Modern footwear also includes a wider range of athletic styles, cushioning, and weather-resistant materials.

Like [[Gloves]], golf shoes connect the player to the equipment and surroundings through a contact surface. The glove supports the connection with the club, while the shoes support contact with the ground. For players who walk the course, comfortable footwear also works alongside lightweight [[Golf Bags]] to make carrying equipment more manageable.
