
# Rangefinders: From Yardage Markers to Digital Measurements

## Estimating Distance Before Electronics

Choosing a golf club depends partly on knowing how far the ball needs to travel. Before handheld electronic devices became common, golfers relied on course markers, yardage books, pacing, and advice from caddies. These methods required players to connect information about the course with their own position. A marker showing the distance to the center of a green, for example, did not necessarily show the exact distance to the flag.

## The Arrival of Laser Technology

Bushnell dates its introduction of golf laser rangefinders to 1995. This development gave golfers a portable way to measure the distance to a selected target. A laser rangefinder sends a light pulse toward an object and uses the returning signal to calculate distance. Golfers can aim at a flag or another visible feature instead of relying entirely on fixed markers.

### Laser Rangefinders and GPS Devices

Laser devices measure a target the golfer can see, while GPS devices use satellite positioning and mapped course information. GPS watches and handheld units can display distances to different parts of a green. These approaches offer different ways to understand the same course.

Accurate distance information supports club selection, but it cannot account for every challenge. Wind, the ball's lie, and the golfer's usual shot distance still matter. A measured yardage is one part of a decision rather than a guarantee of a successful shot.
