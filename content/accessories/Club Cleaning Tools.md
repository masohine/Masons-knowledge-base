## Simple Tools with an Important Purpose

Club maintenance is an everyday part of golf that connects the history of equipment with its continued use. Towels, water, and brushes serve a practical purpose: removing the dirt and grass that accumulate during play. Accessories such as clip on brushes and specialized groove cleaners make these basic tasks easier to perform while moving around the course.

A clubface needs more than a polished appearance. Dirt between the ball and the face can affect contact, and packed grooves cannot work as intended. Cleaning also gives the golfer an opportunity to notice wear or damage before putting the club away.

## A Basic Cleaning Routine

For ordinary modern metal irons, a gentle routine can include these steps:

1. Dampen the clubface with warm water and a little mild soap.
2. Use a soft nylon brush to loosen dirt from the face and grooves.
3. Wipe away residue with a clean damp cloth.
4. Dry the head thoroughly before returning it to the bag.

Manufacturer care instructions should guide the treatment of particular finishes and materials. Aggressive scraping can damage a surface, so a sharper tool is not automatically a better cleaner.

### Preserving Older Clubs

Antique clubs require a different level of care. Wooden heads, older finishes, and historic grips should not automatically receive the same treatment as modern metal irons. 


Keeping a towel and brush accessible in [[Golf Bags]] makes routine maintenance easier. Clean grips also work with [[Gloves]] to support a secure hold, linking equipment care directly to the golfer's experience.
