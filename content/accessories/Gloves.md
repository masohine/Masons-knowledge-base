# Grip, Comfort, and Control

## Why Golfers Wear Gloves

A golf glove adds a layer between the golfer's hand and the club grip. Its main purposes are to improve the security of the grip and reduce friction against the skin. Golfers commonly wear one glove on their lead hand: the left hand for a right handed player and the right hand for a left handed player. Wearing a glove is a personal choice, and some golfers prefer the feel of playing without one.

## Materials and Changing Designs

Golf gloves illustrate how traditional materials and newer manufacturing approaches can serve the same purpose. Leather provides a soft, flexible feel that conforms to the hand. Synthetic materials offer another approach, often emphasizing durability and moisture resistance. Some designs combine leather contact areas with flexible fabric panels to balance feel and movement.

### Gloves for Different Conditions

Specialized rain gloves help maintain a secure hold in wet weather, while winter gloves add warmth. This variety shows how golf accessories have developed to address specific playing conditions rather than offering a single design for every round. The same balance between comfort and performance appears in [[Golf Shoes]].

A good fitting glove should feel snug without restricting movement. Loose material can bunch up, while an overly tight glove can feel uncomfortable. Allowing a glove to dry naturally after play and keeping a spare in [[Golf Bags]] can help golfers prepare for changing conditions. Clean club grips also matter: a glove cannot fully compensate for dirt and oily buildup.
