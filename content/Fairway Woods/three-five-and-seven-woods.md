# Understanding 3-Woods, 5-Woods, and 7-Woods

[[index|Home]] · [[Fairway Woods/index|Fairway Woods]] · [[references|References]]

## What the Numbers Tell You

The number on a fairway wood gives a general indication of its place in a set. Within a typical model family, a 3-wood has less loft and a longer shaft than a 5-wood or 7-wood. Higher-numbered woods usually offer more loft and a shorter playing length. Exact specifications vary, so the number alone does not describe the whole club. Titleist's TS fairway specifications illustrate how loft and length change across a range. [1]

## Different Roles in the Bag

A 3-wood is commonly considered for longer shots, while a 5-wood or 7-wood can offer another way to cover the space between longer woods and irons. PING's high-launch fairway designs also show that helping the ball launch is an explicit design goal, especially for slower swings. [2]

### Compare Results, Not Just Labels

Imagine a golfer who hits a 3-wood low and inconsistently but makes solid contact with a 5-wood. The smaller number does not guarantee a more useful result. A slightly shorter club that produces repeatable shots may have a clearer role in that golfer's bag.

| Club | Common role to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| 3-wood | Longer fairway shots or an alternative tee club |
| 5-wood | A balance of length and launch |
| 7-wood | Higher flight and an option near the long irons |

These are starting points for comparison, not fixed distance promises. The best set arrangement depends on the player's actual shots.
