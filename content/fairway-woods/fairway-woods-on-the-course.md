## More Than a Second-Shot Club

The name fairway wood points toward its use from the ground, but these clubs can also fill a role from the tee. Their usefulness depends on the shot and the lie, meaning how the ball rests. A club that works well from short grass may be much less practical when grass surrounds the ball or an obstacle blocks the intended flight.

## Choose the Shot First

A good way to understand the club is to start with a situation. Imagine a narrow hole where the golfer values a manageable landing area more than the longest possible shot. A fairway wood might be one option. On another hole, the same club might be considered for reaching a distant section of fairway. Neither choice is automatically correct; the player's normal results matter.

### Match the Club to the Task

Useful questions include:

- Is there room for the shot to finish safely?
- Does the ball have a clear lie?
- Is carrying an obstacle more important than total distance?
- Would a shorter club leave a simpler next shot?

PING's fairway fitting discussion distinguishes maximizing distance with longer woods from limiting roll with shorter woods. That distinction supports the idea that different woods can serve different jobs. [1]

The category's history is therefore about versatility as well as distance. Newer construction creates options, while practical decision-making determines whether those options help on a particular hole.
