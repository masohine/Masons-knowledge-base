## Beyond the Wooden Head

Traditional wooden heads and modern hollow heads represent different approaches to making a club. Wood gave earlier clubs their recognizable identity. Later metal and composite designs let manufacturers experiment with thin shells, separate faces, and weight placed in particular parts of the head. This material change is central to understanding why two clubs called woods can look so different. [1]

## Moving Weight Around

PING's G430 MAX fairway design uses a carbon-composite crown and a rear weight. The important idea is that a lighter part of the head can allow weight to be used elsewhere. Manufacturers use these choices to influence stability and ball flight rather than simply making every component as light as possible. [2]

### Design Features Work Together

A clubhead is a system of connected parts. Changing the crown can create options for the sole or rear section, while changing the face can affect another part of performance. That makes it difficult to judge a club by one advertised material alone.

For example, a golfer comparing a traditional wooden club with a hollow modern model should look beyond the finish. The internal construction may be completely different even when both heads have a familiar rounded outline.

> Design takeaway: the location of weight matters as well as the amount of weight.

This original takeaway also connects to cavity-back irons and mallet putters. Across these categories, designers use shape and mass distribution to address the challenge of imperfect contact.
