## Give Each Club a Purpose

A useful golf bag covers a range of shots without filling every space with clubs that perform almost identically. With fairway woods, **distance gapping** means comparing the distances between clubs. The goal is a workable progression, not collecting every available number. Loft, club length, and the golfer's contact all belong in that comparison.

Titleist's TS specifications list several loft choices rather than a single standard fairway wood. PING's high-launch models also show how shaft and total-weight choices can support a particular flight goal. These examples demonstrate why looking at loft alone leaves out part of the design. [1][2]

## A Simple Comparison

Consider a fictional golfer whose two fairway woods carry 175 and 178 yards on typical strikes. Those clubs might overlap too much to justify carrying both. Another arrangement could leave room for a different shot near the long irons. These distances are an illustration, not averages or recommendations.

### Build a Useful Distance Record

1. Compare several ordinary shots with each club.
2. Record carry distance separately from distance after the ball rolls.
3. Notice how widely the shots scatter.
4. Look for clubs that overlap or leave a large uncovered distance.

This approach makes equipment history practical: increasingly specialized designs offer more choices, but the player still needs a reason for each choice. The same thinking applies to arranging irons and wedges.
